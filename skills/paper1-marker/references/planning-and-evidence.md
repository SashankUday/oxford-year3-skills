# Essay-plan and evidence coaching

This is derived coaching informed by Sashank's personal workflow and tutor feedback, not official marking criteria.

## Mind map as the essay plan

Assess the student's map as the plan itself. Look for a defensible answer/thesis, claims organising branches, evidence supporting and challenging claims, connections explaining disagreements, and a conclusion that resolves the question. The original iron-deficiency mind map is a worked planning example; it covers more than the narrower pulmonary tutorial question, so select branches to suit the actual wording.

Give plan strengths, missing argumentative links and a proposed reorganisation of existing branches. Do not award a finished-essay mark or require a separate prose plan. Named studies and citations should be verified before use.

## Evidence that earns its place

Ask what a study does: supports, qualifies, contrasts, establishes a foundational observation, tests mechanism or examines translation. Mechanistic/causal evidence is valuable when the question demands it; descriptive or observational evidence can still be essential. Do not discard a useful paper because a generic weakness is hard to invent.

Useful card fields: study; claim supported; model and method; key finding; what it establishes; what remains uncertain; alternative interpretation or important strength; best essay uses. Add a limitation when there is a substantive one. Never manufacture a “critical sentence” just to fill a template.

Versatility across question families helps preparation. In the actual answer, relevance to this question matters more than general versatility. The tutor examples show the value of precise distinctions: structural proximity versus signalling, association versus causation, disease mechanism versus treatment mechanism, and receptor binding versus functional modulation.

## Personal preparation advice

Sashank suggests an initial pool of roughly 50–60 papers per prepared essay question/family, trimmed towards 20–30 useful papers, with fewer used in the actual exam answer. He recommends around ten full essay-plan mind maps across the four options retained for revision. These are flexible preparation estimates, not mark criteria or quotas. Honour the student's selected options; no default five is prescribed.

Lectures are research starting points. Independent synthesis means choosing and weighing evidence, not inventing a new discovery or simply adding material absent from the lecture. Ask whether extra reading resolves an argumentative need.

Sashank's paper-reading prompt is **“Summarise and critique this paper”**. Use the supplied primary paper and verify its interpretation. Do not assume a named AI tool is available or more reliable.

## Practical paragraph improvement

A useful, flexible sequence is: scientific claim → relevant evidence from complementary/contrasting studies → interpretation of strength or limitation → implication for the answer. Several studies can work within a paragraph without making citation count the objective. Some paragraphs may appropriately develop one decisive experiment in depth.

For a revision demonstration, reorganise the student's verified evidence rather than silently adding new results. Label a hypothetical experiment as proposed and any literature lead as requiring verification.
