# Tutor feedback: four source examples

Read alongside official criteria. These are tutorial judgements, not a representative validation set. Two numerical grades are explicit; no numerical grades were found for the other two. PDF comments were extracted and the rendered pages inspected. Only explicit text comments are interpreted; unlabelled highlights are not assigned invented meanings. Exact comments and page numbers are retained in tutor-annotations.json; personal names and author metadata are omitted.

## 1. Pituitary networks — recorded mark 81%

Source: `Pituitary Networks Essay.pdf` (local source; not bundled), four pages. Question: discuss evidence for pituitary-cell organisation as networks, its relevance to function, and its relation to paracrine signalling. Tutor identity metadata is omitted from the public edition.

**Overall tutor judgement, page 3:** first-class, excellent/outstanding, with experimental evidence and methods, thoughtful appraisal, and original opinion/commentary. The tutor recommended greater depth in places and unpacking sweeping statements.

**Positive comments:** page 1 praises critical insight about the interpretation of calcium imaging; page 2 praises translation/relevance and discussion of evidence; page 3 praises the choice of the FGF1 study for the question.

**Specific improvements:**

- Page 1: anatomical neighbours are not, by themselves, proof of a particular functional route. Explain chemical versus physical connectivity and the evidence for junctions. Later in-vivo imaging should qualify a critique based only on earlier slices.
- Page 2: specify species and the isolation/preparation of somatotrophs when interpreting isolated-cell experiments. Explain separation/characterisation where it affects the inference.
- Page 2: a proposed direct effect on GH needs to be distinguished from indirect effects through cortisol/corticosterone.
- Page 3: limit statements about electrical coupling and generality across cell types to the evidence actually available. The tutor cautions about which cell types had been demonstrated.
- Page 3: future work should connect imaging to intact-animal hormone output and extend beyond one organism; the tutor suggests frequent plasma sampling as an approach.

**Transferable lesson:** strong synthesis and independent judgement can coexist with local scientific corrections. Preserve the successful argument while narrowing unsupported generalisations. Do not re-mark all imperfect essays below 70 automatically.

## 2. Iron deficiency and hypoxia — recorded mark 68%

Source: `Iron Deficiency and Hypoxia -SLL.pdf` (local source; not bundled), four pages. Question: describe, with supporting evidence, mechanisms underlying iron deficiency's effects on pulmonary vascular responses to hypoxia. Annotation author metadata is blank; do not infer identity from the filename.

**Overall tutor judgement, page 4:** the mouse/human evidence and mechanistic links are described well, but clinical implications need greater development: how might manipulating iron improve pulmonary pathophysiology?

**Specific comments:** page 1 asks for precision about HIF-alpha hydroxylation; page 2 points to a Lakhal-Littleton PNAS 2020 report linking endothelin changes to the human study discussed; page 3 cautions that HIF1-alpha is also involved.

These biomedical details are tutor-supplied corrections/leads, not independently verified literature summaries. The essay already contains clinical studies and a treatment-related paragraph: the feedback asks for a stronger mechanistic-to-clinical interpretation, not merely insertion of any clinical content.

**Transferable lesson:** an evidence-rich answer can still underdevelop what the mechanisms imply. Explain what interventions test, the populations/endpoints involved and the limits on moving from a vascular response to patient benefit. Do not treat 68% as proof that the essay lacked all criticism, or transplant all content from the separate iron-deficiency mind map into this narrower question.

## 3. cAMP and depression — qualitative feedback, no recorded numerical mark

Source: `cAMP Depression Essay.pdf` (local source; not bundled), five pages. Question: critically evaluate evidence for involvement of cAMP in depression. Tutor identity metadata is omitted from the public edition.

**Overall tutor judgement, page 4:** good essay; more depth, less breadth and more specific criticism. A critique such as studying both sexes needs an explanation of why it affects inference. Diagrams are suggested.

**Specific comments:** page 1 challenges describing long-established work as recent and asks about limitations of post-mortem studies; page 2 asks how measurements were made and whether the methods introduce problems, and prompts discussion of lipid rafts; page 3 suggests isoform-specific/conditional knockouts rather than treating knockout approaches as a single category. Some interpretations are deferred to tutorial discussion, so do not invent what was subsequently agreed.

**Positive comments:** page 3 approves the distinction between therapeutic efficacy and proof of disease pathophysiology; page 4 calls the proposed spatial-compartmentalisation extension interesting. Other “Good” annotations are preserved in the raw record.

**Transferable lesson:** preserve the causation-versus-treatment distinction and independent proposal, while developing the methods and the implications of limitations. Do not add broad criticism detached from a claim merely to increase the number of critical sentences.

## 4. Taste GPCRs and allostery — qualitative feedback, no recorded numerical mark

Source: `Taste GPCRs Allostery Essay.pdf` (local source; not bundled), six pages. Question: critically discuss evidence for the presence and functional consequences of allosteric GPCR binding sites. Tutor identity metadata is omitted from the public edition.

**Overall tutor judgement, page 6:** readable, but too long for an FHS essay; parts resemble a mini-review/book chapter. Develop depth instead of peripheral breadth. Organise a paragraph around a scientific topic and cite several studies, rather than one paper per paragraph.

**Specific comments:** page 1 requests a sentence signposting the essay's main moves and a sketch of selected data; page 2 includes a positive comment; page 6 requests a bibliography for the FHS essay.

**Transferable lesson:** readability, many studies and appended caveats do not ensure an argumentative answer. Preserve useful comparisons, then group evidence by the inference it supports (for example, distinguishing binding-site evidence from downstream functional effects). Signpost the scope and be selective. Do not turn the tutor's suggestions into mandatory diagrams, a literal “In this essay I will” sentence, or a fixed exam word limit.

## Cross-example priorities

1. Select evidence to answer the wording, not to display everything learned.
2. Explain methods enough to support or limit the inference.
3. Make criticisms study-specific and consequential.
4. Integrate studies around scientific claims and adjudicate disagreement.
5. Distinguish mechanistic plausibility, physiological relevance and clinical benefit where relevant.
6. Reward independent, evidence-based judgement and acknowledge strengths.

The recorded 81% and 68% are anchors illustrating tutor judgements. They do not establish model accuracy, represent every topic, or license claiming a calibrated marking system.
